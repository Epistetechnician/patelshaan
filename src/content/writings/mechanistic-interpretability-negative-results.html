Interpretability: One Narrow Yes and Many Honest Noes

When you stare long enough at activation heatmaps and feature dashboards, it becomes almost irresistibly easy to see faces in the clouds. Humans are meaning-making creatures; hand us a glowing cluster of neurons and three suggestive prompts, and our imagination will happily draft a mechanism before the experiment even finishes. Trying to build mechanistic interpretability on real models under gates locked in advance turned out to be an exercise in resisting that exact romance, and what came back was one quiet, narrow yes surrounded by a long stretch of honest noes.

The one instrument that passed

Our lone clean positive in this initial sequence was a Gemma 3 trace qualification. Pooled NMSE landed at 0.0457 against a pre-committed 0.05 ceiling, with parity, repeatability, no-op stability, intervention reach, event replay, and feature stability all passing. Notice what that actually gives us, and what it refuses to promise. It tells us our measuring probe behaves cleanly on one specific model, one runtime, and one learned dictionary. It does not hand us a causal circuit diagram or a window into machine introspection.

Learning to honor the stop sign

Every step that tried to cross from instrument qualification into stronger causal claims ran straight into our pre-committed walls, and watching those runs halt taught me more than a tidy triumph would have:

  • Gemma causal feature effects (V2). Tune sign agreement came in at 0.75 against a locked 0.80 bar, alongside one failed Holm-corrected gate. By design, the held-out split stayed unopened: refusing to burn clean data trying to rescue a method that had already stumbled on tune.
  • Astral V42–V46 and V48. Target reliability, localization, measurement invariance, response-anchored and answer-aligned targets, and cross-view transport all closed NoCandidate. Instead of poking extra layers, wrappers, or corpora until something flickered green, the line stopped.
  • GPT-2 MLP-out qualification. Closed cleanly and archived as negative evidence; the residual-stream follow-up began from a fresh packet and corpus without quietly reopening the failed slice.

At first, closing six experiments in a row feels bleak. Then a deeper clarity settles in: stopping is the scientific contribution. Search across enough layers, prompt templates, and thresholds and something will eventually cross a cutoff by sheer luck. Deciding in advance when you will walk away is the only thing separating real discovery from polished storytelling.

Neurons as costumes: the permutation shock

The sharpest conceptual shift actually arrived from a neighboring project, yet it cut straight through how I read every interpretability paper afterward. Taking a GPT-2 MLP, I applied a function-preserving permutation that relocated 100% of the selected parameter positions while keeping top-1 model behavior virtually identical (mean relative logit error of 3e-7). When I ran the original ablation by raw coordinate label, the causal effect looked utterly flipped (cosine −0.983); when I mapped the ablation through the inverse permutation, agreement snapped right back to 0.9999996.

Pause and feel what that means. Raw coordinates carry no intrinsic identity. Saying "neuron 417 encodes concept X" only makes sense inside a declared coordinate frame, and a feature that seems to migrate or dissolve across training checkpoints may simply have changed chairs. Every causal mechanism claim deserves to be framed as a representation-relative statement, and a simple permutation check ought to sit in every lab’s standard control kit.

Copying answers while missing how the surface bends

A companion finding deepened that humility. When I fitted a learned replacement for an MLP block, the substitute matched output values fairly closely (nRMSE around 0.10 to 0.12) while missing local derivatives dramatically (Jacobian nRMSE 0.88 to 0.89), even right along the training directions. A module can echo what another module says at rest without sharing how it responds when leaned on. Yet interventions, fine-tuning, and real-world distribution shifts are all pushes, which means local sensitivity is the exact quantity safety actually depends on. And in another control, dropping in random and zero blocks preserved 100% task accuracy, a blunt reminder that a test a blank circuit passes isn’t measuring the mechanism you thought it was.

Building a sandbox that refuses to flatter us

What remains reusable from all those stops is the deterministic interpretability sandbox itself: native-versus-instrumented parity checks, feature encoding and reconstruction, intervention reach, checkpoint stability, causal scrubbing, hash-chained circuit lineage, matched-data accounting for continual-learning controllers, and fail-closed custody gates. Live model execution stays off by default, outputs remain aggregate-only, and any run halts on the first failed fixed gate.

Alongside that sits an early linguistics lab built around a quiet suspicion: asking a language model in English to explain its own mind tells you as much about its post-training etiquette as about its internals. That lane explores discovery evaluators that bypass verbal self-explanation: running from pinned, license-checked sources through deterministic fixtures and adversarial evaluators, though it remains strictly internal development with no corpus, weights, or external verdict yet. I mention it to share the direction of curiosity, not to claim a result.

In the end, interpretability earns a seat at the safety table only when a discovered feature predicts what happens the moment you push on it out of sample. Until it clears that held-out bar, it is a beautiful visualization rather than a safety tool. We are leaving this round with no causal self-model and no universal method, just one qualified probe on one stack, a handful of sharpened controls, and a healthier respect for how patiently reality makes us earn real understanding.
