What We Know and Still Don't: A Research Map

Think of this essay as a walk through a cluttered workshop with all the drawers left half open. Over the past year I have been orbiting six tightly knotted questions: how an AI system keeps learning without forgetting who it promised to be, what lives inside its weights, how to hold an autonomous agent accountable, and how ordinary communities might verify the compute and cryptography beneath them. I left the shattered prototypes on the workbench alongside the pieces that held, because the dead ends are usually the honest map the next person actually needs.

Why the ceiling sits beside every bench

Working solo with powerful tools makes it dangerously easy to impress yourself. Early on I realized I needed a house rule stronger than my own optimism. Every experiment in this map binds its claim to an exact state slice, a frozen protocol, and a ceiling, from a humble design note at the bottom to an independently reproduced result at the top. A clean pass on synthetic data qualifies a harness; it never proves a hypothesis about the world. A replay on my own laptop is a sanity check, never external replication. And while a model is welcome to propose ideas or drafts, it never approves its own evidence, widens its own permissions, edits its own judge, or promotes its own weights.

Read everything in these notes with that gentle warning in mind: most of the work still rests on the lower rungs: bounded, local, and unreplicated by a second pair of hands. If you want the full philosophical and empirical arc in one sitting, the flagship essay Evidence-Bounded Intelligence gathers these six benches into one continuous argument.

Six rooms in the same house

At first these six directions felt like separate projects; slowly they revealed themselves as adjoining rooms with the doors removed.

Continual learning and alignment benchmarks ask how a living system stays faithful to its values while adapting. Here a synthetic harness passed cleanly, a matched-progress check exposed an illusion where "safer" simply meant "learned less," and most real-model trials ended in honest NoCandidate stops. Right beside it, hybrid synthetic-data systems treat synthetic generators as provenance-bound factories kept on a short leash: useful for coverage and rehearsal, yet strictly forbidden from certifying themselves without real observations that can contradict them.

Step into mechanistic interpretability and nonlinguistic representations and the tone gets quieter still: one narrow measurement qualification on Gemma 3, a long row of terminal negative results where pre-committed stop rules prevented cherry-picking, and a sobering permutation demonstration showing why raw neuron coordinates cannot anchor causal identity. From understanding, the walk naturally turns to action in verifiable AI agents: building a small Rust control plane where an untrusted proposal earns every action through evidence, deterministic admission, a single-use capability, and a sandbox, backed by 279 passing tests and a single hardware-attested TDX run.

Beneath agents sit the deeper foundations: MPC, zero-knowledge proofs, and post-quantum cryptography, where I have the least finished ground to claim and say so openly: a semantics-first ZK benchmark generator, a formal-evidence ladder that refuses to bluff, and the quantum-circuit bounds behind ECDSA.fail: and finally decentralized compute and data infrastructure, prototyping outcome protocols, replay-safe settlement, tamper-evident run logs, and lineage envelopes so a community can verify computation it never ran itself.

Notice how each room hands its unanswered tension to the next. Questions about learning and internal representation feed straight into whether safety holds tomorrow; the instant a system acts in the world, safety becomes a question of bounded authority; and authority only endures as long as the cryptography and shared infrastructure underneath it cannot be quietly rewritten. Every layer borrows humility from the layer before it.

What the work taught me to keep

Looking back over the year, five quiet habits outlasted every single benchmark table. First, terminal negative results turned out to be the primary harvest, and refusing to hide them made the rare positives worth trusting. Second, infrastructure and tooling breakdowns cost more phases than scientific hypotheses did: like an interpretability lane halted outright because a pinned dependency lacked a build for the host architecture, and logging them cleanly as stops rather than scientific failures kept us from mistaking broken plumbing for a refuted idea.

Third, a fail-closed refusal to promote weak evidence feels small in a headline and priceless in a real system. Fourth, narrow claims carry more dignity than inflated ones, because anyone can pick them up without getting burned. And fifth, measurement itself is almost always the softest board in the floor: watching two graders disagree by more than a factor of three over the exact same model outputs cured me of reading any single safety percentage at face value.

How the map grew in November 2026

Between the first sketch of this map and November 2026, seven public repositories moved several of these questions onto pre-registered, recomputable ground: Holdout Ledger priced adaptive holdout leakage in bits across 59,200 confirmatory runs (9/9 hypotheses passing, 0 certificate violations); FormaSec aligned Python, Rust, Kani, and Lean 4 admission kernels across 600 structural cases with 0 unsafe admissions in 552 hostile proposals; Caduceus lifted governed agent skill resolution by +0.245 on Haiku and +0.305 on Sonnet; Playbox paired multi-owner Biscuit rooms with an H100 F3 pilot that caught a planted dev-set memorizer; Mechanistic Interpretability Unified replicated Qwen3-1.7B R6 agreement units (p = 0.000999) while proving rules caught 0/8 planted flaws, probes caught 3/8, and J-space tied norm-matched PCA across OLMo-2 and Tulu-3; Temporal Grid Safety showed frozen discriminant adapters holding 0.95 vs 0.27 refusals under benign OOD drift after we caught our own attacker-rate confound; and HSI Lab separated +0.640 typed codecs and +0.238 verified synthetic gains from a −0.0025 null on 7B code. All twelve repositories and the Mount Kailash corpus are now browsable directly in the research monograph.

Nothing on this map pretends to be an official external leaderboard, a production certification, or an independent replication unless a page explicitly documents that bar. Consider it an open field notebook from one traveler to another, and if you spot a crack I walked past, I would always rather hear it from you today than build on top of it tomorrow.
