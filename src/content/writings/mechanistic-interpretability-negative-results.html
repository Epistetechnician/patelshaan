Interpretability: One Narrow Yes and Many Honest Noes

If we want to know how a model arrives at an output, we have to read its internal representations and test whether what we read is causal. I tried, on real models, under gates committed in advance. The result is one narrow positive and a long run of terminal negatives, and I think the shape of that result is more useful than a cleaner story would be.

The one positive

A Gemma 3 trace qualification passed. Pooled NMSE was 0.0457 against a 0.05 gate, and parity, repeatability, no-op, intervention reach, event replay, and feature stability all passed. That is a measurement qualification for a specific model, runtime, and set of learned features. It shows the instrument is trustworthy on that stack. It is not a causal graph and not evidence of introspection.

The negatives, in order of how much they taught me

  • Causal feature effects (Gemma, V2). Tune sign agreement was 0.75 against a 0.80 gate, and one Holm-corrected gate failed. The held-out set was never opened, by design, so it could not be spent on a method that had already failed its tuning check.
  • Astral V42 to V46 and V48. Target reliability, localization, measurement invariance, response-anchored and answer-aligned targets, and cross-view transport all ended NoCandidate. The program stopped instead of searching more layers, wrappers, or corpora.
  • MLP-out qualification on GPT-2. Closed, and kept as historical negative evidence. The follow-up on the residual stream uses a fresh packet and corpus and does not reopen the failed slice.

Stopping is the finding. With enough layers, wrappers, and corpora, something will clear a threshold. Committing in advance to stop is what keeps that something from being noise.

A result about the unit of analysis

The most surprising thing I found came from a different project, but it bears directly on interpretability. A function-preserving permutation of a GPT-2 MLP changed 100% of the selected parameter positions while preserving the model's top-1 behavior, with a mean relative logit error of 3e-7. The same-label ablation then looked unrelated to the original (cosine similarity -0.983), while the inverse-mapped ablation matched almost exactly (0.9999996).

The implication is that raw coordinates are not a stable identity for a mechanism. A claim like "this neuron does X" is only meaningful relative to a representation, and a feature that appears to move between checkpoints may simply have been relabeled. I think many mechanism claims need to be restated as claims about representation-relative quantities, and that a permutation test should be a standard control.

Matching outputs is not matching mechanism

A second result points the same way. When I fit a replacement for an MLP block, the fitted module matched values reasonably well (nRMSE about 0.10 to 0.12) but missed local derivatives badly (Jacobian nRMSE 0.88 to 0.89), and the error stayed large even along the training directions. A module can copy what another module outputs without copying how the output would change if you pushed on it. Interventions, fine-tuning, and distribution shift are all pushes, which is why the derivative is the thing safety cares about.

A related control: random and zero blocks retained 100% task accuracy in one setting. If a null model passes your behavioral test, the test is not measuring the mechanism you think it is.

The harness is the contribution

The interpretability sandbox is a deterministic harness for the interfaces this work needs: parity between native and instrumented models, feature encoding and reconstruction, intervention reach, checkpoint feature stability, causal scrubbing, circuit lineage with hash-chained transitions, matched-data accounting for continual-learning controllers, and custody gates that fail closed. Real execution is disabled by default, aggregate-only, and closes on the first failed fixed gate.

Nonlinguistic representations

Language is a poor probe for what a model represents, because the model also learns to talk about itself in language, and its account of itself is evidence about its training as much as about its internals. A linguistics lab sits beside the interpretability work to test representations and discovery evaluators that do not route through the model's own explanations. Its gate model runs from pinned sources and license custody through deterministic fixtures, semantic checks, and an adversarial evaluator to an internal-pass receipt, and then to qualification for external review. It is at internal-development-only status, with no corpus, no weights, and no external verdict. I include it to show the direction, not because it has a finding.

Why this matters for safety

Interpretability is only useful to safety if it survives contact with a held-out test. If a method that finds a feature cannot predict what happens when you intervene on it, you have a visualization, not a safety tool. Most of the candidate methods I tried were still visualizations when the held-out gate arrived.

What this does not establish

No causal self-model, no general interpretability method, and no introspection claim. One instrument was qualified on one stack, and a few controls are offered for other people to reuse.
