Proofs, Privacy, and the Quantum Clock

If AI infrastructure is going to be trusted without trusting its operator, it needs privacy-preserving computation, verifiable interactions, and cryptography that survives a quantum computer. This is the area where I have the least to show, so I will separate carefully what exists from what is only a direction.

Zero-knowledge: a benchmark that starts from meaning

Most ZK benchmarks compare known circuits. Composed ZK Benchmark OS starts from semantics: state machines, transitions, guards, loops, invariants, and expected accept or reject outcomes. It generates workloads across nine families, applies 14 classes of adversarial mutation with expected verdicts, replays them locally, and scores performance separately from soundness evidence.

The reasoning is that a fast backend that accepts a mutated, invalid trace is worse than a slow one that rejects it, and a speed leaderboard hides the difference. Claims are leveled from a design note up to an independently reproduced result. Everything here is Level 1: local, deterministic infrastructure. No official benchmark or backend performance result exists. An oracle-completeness audit and a distinguishability matrix show which generated constructs the shipped oracle can and cannot evaluate, which I consider a feature of the design.

The formal ladder, and what it refuses to claim

On the formal side there is one real local Z3 unsat result for a tiny digest-binding obligation, an implementation-diverse checker that agreed on a golden vector, 18 mutations, opposite set orders, and 256 generated cases, and a Lean kernel-checked theorem about a handwritten model of the checker. None of that proves the Rust source or the hash function.

I also tried Rust-to-Lean extraction with Aeneas across roughly 80 phases. Extraction ran, but no complete kernel-checked witness resulted. The attempts ended in tooling, environment, and execution-protocol stops, each recorded and cleaned up. The lasting product was a fail-closed execution state machine and a 102-command source ledger that make such attempts auditable. The finding is that protocol discipline dominated cost, and that the accepted-evidence path still structurally blocks formal claims it has not earned.

Attestation as a stopgap for privacy

I have no working MPC protocol to report. What I have is hardware attestation, which solves part of the same trust problem differently. One agent-maintenance run on a TDX confidential-compute instance was verified against a signed attestation covering the image digest, source revision, and security state. That narrows who must be trusted. It is not MPC, and it still leaves the hardware vendor in the trust base.

I also keep a copy of a public homomorphic-encryption bounty artifact for study. The first version of that challenge was withdrawn because an unnecessary commitment let people check plaintext guesses offline. I include it as a reminder that a published artifact can leak an unintended oracle. I have no result on it.

The quantum clock: ECDSA.fail

Elliptic-curve signatures are the reason post-quantum migration has a clock. ECDSA.Fail is a public, evaluator-verified research loop for improving reversible secp256k1 point-addition circuits, a costly primitive inside Shor's algorithm. The arXiv paper reports an 86.1% reduction in the score participants optimized, with a best Q × T score of 1.496 billion at the paper's July cutoff. A later 1.259 billion score is reported separately, and its coherent windowed variant is a per-call sensitivity model, not a full-Shor success estimate. Comparisons use different interfaces and accounting conventions, so the numbers should not be lined up as a ranking.

For security planning the takeaway is directional: cheaper circuits shorten the estimated distance to a cryptographically relevant quantum computer. That is a reason to plan migration early, not a prediction of a date.

Post-quantum cryptography: honest status

I have not implemented or benchmarked a post-quantum scheme in these repositories. My contribution is the threat estimate and the evidence discipline, not a replacement primitive. One direction I would like to explore, and have not begun, is applying the semantics-first, adversarial-mutation approach to post-quantum signature verification inside agent receipts, so that receipts stay checkable when classical signatures stop being safe.

A quantum-adjacent negative

MeshQRC tested a quantum reservoir computing lane for industrial anomaly detection, with a rule that live inference stays classical. On a synthetic regime dataset a tuned local QRC matched the best validation PR AUC and F1 and led on test PR AUC. On the non-synthetic proxies (a server-machine dataset and an industrial-control dataset) it trailed the strongest classical baselines on key metrics, so further remote QRC work was deferred. Quantum is not free signal, and the benchmark was built to say so.

What this does not establish

No ZK benchmark result, no MPC implementation, no post-quantum implementation, and no formal proof of the Rust code.
